Four-wing / dual-pair

A different machine: elongated boom, two drive stations, four slender plates. You buy yaw/roll authority and accept the DOF and mass tax on day one.

Why not Flyer-0 leftovers

Adding two plates to a two-wing crank does not make a dual-pair flyer. Fore and hind need their own drive, phase, and structure from the first sketch.

What this is not

Not Phase-2 of Flyer-0. Not “add two wings later.” Neighbor starts: Flyer-0 (two-wing rotary + spring) and Flyer-2 (piezo / resonant flexure, no crank).

Path to prototype

A dual-pair path. Public cards only: phase + one-line + qualitative exit.

P0 — Pair architecture · open

Boom length, station spacing, and two-drive layout frozen as a start — not as a Flyer-0 add-on.

Exit: fore and hind are specified as independent machines that share a boom.

P1 — Dual bench

Two surrogate pairs on a rigid beam. Prove phase offset without asking for lift.

Exit: both pairs run, and you can hold a chosen phase.

P2 — Coupled tether

Four wings on one body, soft tether. Watch boom torsion and pair interference.

Exit: tethered flap that does not tear a hinge or twist the boom apart.

P3 — Authority hops

Indoor/netted. First question is whether pair disagreement steers, not just whether it hops.

Exit: an intended heading change, not only an intended hop.

P4 — Demo ready

Repeatable dual-pair research demo.

Exit: someone else can run the pair-phase checklist.
