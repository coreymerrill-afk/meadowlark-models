A frame table for the X1 Ultra

Two long 2×6 rails, two end rails, six joists on 16 in centers, six 4×4 legs, a lower 2×4 ring, two plywood end panels, and a three-piece plywood top. You and a helper build each long side flat on the floor, stand the sides up, and tie the ends. Each side frame is about 50 lb. The machine’s long walls are heavier: three people on a wall, and a lift table if one is over about 150 lb. The 12 ft boards need a longer vehicle or a delivery.

Why it does not sway or sag

The machine and the bed sit on the plywood, the plywood sits on joists every 16 inches, the joists sit in two long rails, and those rails are bolted to six legs that run straight down to the levelers. Why the middle legs matter: the long rails are only strong enough with the middle legs bearing; do not skip them. Each plywood seam lands on a joist center, so every sheet edge has wood under it. The end panels and the lower 2×4 ring keep the ends from leaning, and the plywood ties the top into one sheet. When it is standing, push the deck sideways with about 50 lb. Add a pair of panels on the long sides only if you can see or feel it lean, or the level bubble moves. Repeat that push once the machine is on.

The laser bed

The steel egg-crate has its own Bed tab: two interlocking grids, no fasteners, and the cut files. The honeycomb in these steps is the thin stack until that bed is cut.

Keep the bed stack thin. A honeycomb tile is a metal grid, 22 mm thick, with cells about 5.5, 7.5, or 9.5 mm across. It is usually framed. A steel grid is magnetic, so a magnet can hold a sheet. The open cells let the beam pass and give smoke a way down. Honeycomb is not a fire break by itself, so a 1.5 mm steel plate sits under it. Together that is about 24 mm. The machine’s Z travel is about 80 mm.

A single panel the size of the work area, 1250 × 2450 mm, is not a realistic buy. Use two tiles, about 4 × 4 ft each, meeting at the center. Together the plates and the tiles are about 96 × 48 in, slightly smaller than ACMER's 1250 × 2450 mm work area. The tiles are made to order, so order them before Day 1. Order the plates before Day 1 too.

The six levelers level the deck. After the tiles are on, check the head height at the corners and the middle, and shim a tile corner until the spread is about 1 mm.

The CNC waste board is later: two pieces of ¾ in MDF, about 48 × 48 in, screwed along the joist lines. The tiles and the plates lift out for CNC and for cleaning. Buy the MDF on CNC day.

The machine is 2834 × 1630 × 341 mm (111.6 × 64.2 × 13.4 in) and about 550 lb. It has an open bottom and no feet. This deck is 113.5 × 66 in, so the ring sits fully on the plywood, about 0.95 in in from each end and 0.9 in in from each long side.

Tools: a circular saw and a 4 ft straightedge, a drill with a long ½ bit and a Torx bit, a wrench, a level, clamps, shim stock, and sawhorses. A table saw is not required.

Shop & cut list

A 2×4 is 1½ × 3½, a 2×6 is 1½ × 5½, a 4×4 is 3½ × 3½, and ¾ plywood is 23/32, about 0.72 in thick. The frame is a mirror image left to right and front to back.

Legs are 32.78 in if the leveler is 2.5 in. Dry-stack your real leveler before cutting. At that length only two legs fit in an 8 ft 4×4, so buy 3 posts.

    About the bed
    Corner

    Steel egg-crate bed

    Two steel grids sit on the deck, inside the opening. The work rests on the tooth tips, with open air underneath. Steel takes diode, infrared, and CNC use. Blades notch up from the bottom, ribs notch down from the top, and they drop together. No bolts and no welds. Confirm the size after the inside opening is measured.

    Slot and tooth sizes
    • Blades run the long way, 50 mm apart, 38 mm tall including the teeth. Teeth are 4 mm tall on a 12 mm pitch.
    • Ribs run across, 183.3 mm apart, the first and last 50 mm in from the blade ends. A rib is 34 mm tall, so only the teeth touch the work.
    • Slots are 3.4 mm wide and 17.3 mm deep. The steel is 1/8 in, 3.18 mm, so a slot is the thickness plus 0.2 mm.

    Floor and a twisted board

    • Point loads from the levelers are fine on the coated slab. Pads go under every foot.
    • A twisted 2×6 can be a joist, crown up, if the screws pull it to the line. Do not make it a long rail.
    • If your leveler is not 2.5 in at mid-travel, recut the legs. Leg length is the top height, minus 0.72 in of plywood, minus the leveler you measured.
    • After about a month, check the level and tighten the bolts. The bed can be shimmed if the top is a little off.

    Changing the height

    The plywood top is one number in this file: TOP_H = 36, near the top of the script. Leg length, the end panels, and the 4×4 count follow it. The 24 mm bed stack puts the work surface about an inch higher.
