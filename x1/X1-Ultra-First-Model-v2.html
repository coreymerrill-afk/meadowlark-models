X1 Ultra stand

A symmetric frame table. Build the two long sides on the floor, then stand them up.

A frame table for the X1 Ultra

Two long 2×6 rails, two end rails, six joists on 16 in centers, six 4×4 legs, a lower 2×4 ring, two plywood end panels, and a three-piece plywood top. You and a helper build each long side flat on the floor, stand the sides up, and tie the ends. Each side frame is about 50 lb. The machine’s long walls are heavier: three people on a wall, and a lift table if one is over about 150 lb. The 12 ft boards need a longer vehicle or a delivery.

Why it does not sway or sag

The machine and the bed sit on the plywood, the plywood sits on joists every 16 inches, the joists sit in two long rails, and those rails are bolted to six legs that run straight down to the levelers. Why the middle legs matter: the long rails are only strong enough with the middle legs bearing; do not skip them. Each plywood seam lands on a joist center, so every sheet edge has wood under it. The end panels and the lower 2×4 ring keep the ends from leaning, and the plywood ties the top into one sheet. When it is standing, push the deck sideways with about 50 lb. Add a pair of panels on the long sides only if you can see or feel it lean, or the level bubble moves. Repeat that push once the machine is on.

The laser bed

The bed is two 1.5 mm steel fire plates, each 1200 × 1200 mm, with two laser-cut steel egg-crate panels on top. Each panel is 1200 × 1200 mm, 24 blades and 7 ribs, 38 mm tall. The blades and ribs interlock, and nothing is fastened between them. The Bed tab has the model and the cut files.

The stack is about 40 mm: 38 mm of egg-crate on a 1.5 mm plate. Z travel is about 80 mm. It is not yet known whether focus height is measured from the machine bottom, so a way to raise or lower the machine may be needed. That question is still open with ACMER.

Lay the two plates on the open frame and fix them to it. Set the two egg-crate panels on top, side by side, and fasten them through the bottom or with semi-permanent clips. Nothing is fastened in the work area.

The six levelers level the deck. After the panels are on, check the head height at the corners and the middle.

The CNC waste board is later: two pieces of ¾ in MDF, about 48 × 48 in, screwed along the joist lines. The panels and the plates come off for CNC and for cleaning. Buy the MDF on CNC day.

The machine is 2834 × 1630 × 341 mm (111.6 × 64.2 × 13.4 in) and about 550 lb. It has an open bottom and no feet. This deck is 113.5 × 66 in, so the ring sits fully on the plywood, about 0.95 in in from each end and 0.9 in in from each long side.

Tools: a circular saw and a 4 ft straightedge, a drill with a long ½ bit and a Torx bit, a wrench, a level, clamps, shim stock, and sawhorses. A table saw is not required.

Shop & cut list

A 2×4 is 1½ × 3½, a 2×6 is 1½ × 5½, a 4×4 is 3½ × 3½, and ¾ plywood is 23/32, about 0.72 in thick. The frame is a mirror image left to right and front to back.

Legs are 32.78 in if the leveler is 2.5 in. Dry-stack your real leveler before cutting. At that length only two legs fit in an 8 ft 4×4, so buy 3 posts.

    About the bed
    Whole bed

    Steel egg-crate bed

    Each panel is 24 blades and 7 ribs. The bed is two panels, 48 blades and 14 ribs. Blades and ribs are the same 1/8 in steel. Two greys keep the crossings easy to see.

    The two panels sit on the deck, inside the opening, 2400 × 1200 mm together. The work rests on the tooth tips. Vents in the bottom edge, 20 mm tall and 120 mm wide with rounded corners, let air and debris pass between cells. Two 1.5 mm steel plates sit under the panels. Steel takes diode, infrared, and CNC use. Blades notch up from the bottom, ribs notch down from the top, and they drop together. No bolts and no welds. Confirm the size after the inside opening is measured.

    Slot and tooth sizes
    • Blades run the long way, 50 mm apart, 38 mm tall including the teeth. Teeth are 4 mm tall on a 12 mm pitch.
    • Ribs run across, 183.3 mm apart, the first and last 50 mm in from the blade ends. A rib is 34 mm tall, so only the teeth touch the work.
    • Slots are 3.4 mm wide and 17.3 mm deep. The steel is 1/8 in, 3.18 mm, so a slot is the thickness plus 0.2 mm.
    • Each blade has six vents in the bottom edge, 120 mm wide and 20 mm tall, with rounded corners about 6 mm across, centered between the ribs.
    Machine lift

    Machine lift

    The steel bed stays on the table. Fourteen actuators raise only the machine.

    How the lift works

    The two fire plates and the two egg-crate panels are fixed on the deck. The ACMER X1 Ultra has no feet and a flat underside. Fourteen small electric actuators, mounted to the table frame from below, push up on flat steel pads under the machine skirting. That raises the whole machine off the bed and gives more room above the grid, including when an MDF board is on it. The plates and the egg-crate do not move.

    Five actuators sit on each long side, corners included, and two more sit on each end. Four steel stops at the corners keep the machine from sliding sideways. Each actuator is a 12 V screw with a 100 mm (4 in) stroke. A limit switch stops it at the bottom and at the top. The screw is self-locking: it holds the load when the power is off. A level sensor reads tilt. An ESP32 controller board, on the shop Wi-Fi, serves the phone screen.

    A height change is rare. Start with every actuator on its bottom stop. Run them all up at the same time, by the same amount. Then trim one at a time, using the level sensor, until the machine is level. The self-locking screws hold that height. The lift does not lift the bed.

    Floor and a twisted board

    • Point loads from the levelers are fine on the coated slab. Pads go under every foot.
    • A twisted 2×6 can be a joist, crown up, if the screws pull it to the line. Do not make it a long rail.
    • If your leveler is not 2.5 in at mid-travel, recut the legs. Leg length is the top height, minus 0.72 in of plywood, minus the leveler you measured.
    • After about a month, check the level and tighten the bolts. The bed can be shimmed if the top is a little off.

    Changing the height

    The plywood top is one number in this file: TOP_H = 36, near the top of the script. Leg length, the end panels, and the 4×4 count follow it. The 40 mm bed puts the work surface about 1.6 in higher.
