A frame table for the X1 Ultra

Two long 2×6 rails, two end rails, six joists on 16 in centers, six 4×4 legs, a lower 2×4 ring, two plywood end panels, and a three-piece plywood top. You and a helper build each long side flat on the floor, stand the sides up, and tie the ends. The heaviest lift is about 50 lb.

Why it does not sway or sag

The machine and the bed sit on the plywood, the plywood sits on joists every 16 inches, the joists sit in two long rails, and those rails are bolted to six legs that run straight down to the levelers. Each plywood seam lands on a joist center, so every sheet edge has wood under it. The end panels and the lower 2×4 ring keep the ends from leaning, and the plywood ties the top into one sheet. When it is standing, push the deck sideways with about 50 lb; add a pair of panels on the long sides only if it sways more than about 1/32 in.

The laser bed

A honeycomb tile is a thin metal grid, about 22 mm thick, with cells about 5.5, 7.5, or 9.5 mm across. It is usually framed. A steel grid is magnetic, so a magnet can hold a sheet. The open cells let the beam pass and give smoke a way down. Honeycomb is not a fire break by itself, so a loose metal plate sits under it.

A single panel the size of the work area, 1250 × 2450 mm, is not a realistic buy. Use two tiles, about 4 × 4 ft each, meeting at the center. Made-to-order tiles take time, so order them early.

The six levelers level the deck. After the tiles are on, check the head height at the corners and the middle, and shim a tile corner until the spread is about 1 mm.

The CNC waste board is later: two pieces of ¾ in MDF, about 48 × 48 in, screwed along the joist lines. The tiles and the plates lift out for CNC and for cleaning. Buy the MDF on CNC day.

ACMER’s own bed, feet, and weight are still unknown. The outline, the doublers, and the tile thickness in the picture are provisional.

Tools: a circular saw and a straightedge, a drill with a long ⅜ bit and a Torx bit, a wrench, a level, clamps, and sawhorses. A table saw is not required.

Shop & cut list

A 2×4 is 1½ × 3½, a 2×6 is 1½ × 5½, a 4×4 is 3½ × 3½, and ¾ plywood is 23/32, about 0.72 in thick. The frame is a mirror image left to right and front to back.

Legs are 32.78 in if the leveler is 2.5 in. Dry-stack your real leveler before cutting. At that length only two legs fit in an 8 ft 4×4, so buy 3 posts.

    Floor and a twisted board

    • Point loads from the levelers are fine on the coated slab. Pads go under every foot.
    • A twisted 2×6 can be a joist, crown up, if the screws pull it to the line. Do not make it a long rail.
    • If your leveler is not 2.5 in at mid-travel, recut the legs. Leg length is the top height, minus 0.72 in of plywood, minus the leveler you measured.
    • After about a month, check the level and tighten the bolts. The bed can be shimmed if the top is a little off.

    Changing the height

    The plywood top is one number in this file: TOP_H = 36, near the top of the script. Leg length, the end panels, and the 4×4 count follow it. The work surface sits about an inch higher once the plate and the honeycomb are on.
