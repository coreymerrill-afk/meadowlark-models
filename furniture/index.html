Nightstand

A bedside table from one sheet of 18 mm plywood. It stands on its own feet, and the same sheet includes a French cleat backer so it can hang on the wall.

Dimensions

Overall 560 × 400 × 600 mm (22 × 15.75 × 23.6 in)
Top 560 × 400 mm, overhanging the carcass 20 mm on every side
Carcass 520 mm wide × 360 mm deep. Drawer above an open cubby.
Parts 15 plywood parts in the model: 13 in the standing piece, 2 optional cleat layers. Two test coupons are in the nest and are not drawn here.
Sheet One 1220 × 2440 mm sheet. The parts cover 49% of it.
Thickness 18 mm plywood. Slots are drawn 0.1 mm over the tab.

The sheet

Build

Glue is the joint. Spread wood glue on every tab, slot, and finger face, then drive the screws so they clamp the joint while the glue cures. The screws stay in after that. Scrape or sand the char off the lasered faces first, because char does not hold glue. Wipe the squeeze-out. Give it about an hour before you move it, and a day before you load it.

Pilot holes are 3.0 mm. Thirty screws in all: twenty #8 × 1½ in, and six #8 × 1¼ in. The four #8 × 1¾ in are only for the cleat backer. The shorter 1¼ in screws are the ones that must not poke out the far side (runners and drawer face).

  1. Dry-fit the parts. Lay one side flat, glue the back and the shelf into its slots, fit the other side, and screw from the outside.
  2. Turn the carcass onto its back and screw both runners on. They need to sit level with each other, or the drawer will rack.
  3. Lay the top upside down. Stand the carcass on it so the side tabs enter the top slots, glue, and drive the four top screws. Check that all four feet touch the floor.
  4. If you are adding the cleat, do it now, before the drawer goes in. Glue the two backer layers to the rear of the back panel and drive the four screws from inside. Then screw the Z-bar to the outer layer.
  5. Glue the drawer finger joints and the bottom tabs, check the box is square, and drive the box screws. Glue and screw the face on from the inside. Leave the drawer free of glue so it can ride on the runners.
Cut the two test coupons first. The tab should slide through the slot with finger pressure and sit flush. Adjust the cut from that pair before you cut the real parts. A feed of 100–200 mm per minute at full power is only a starting guess. The machine’s maker does not publish a rate for 18 mm plywood, so treat that speed as an unverified estimate and set your own from the coupons. Stay with the machine while it cuts.

Notes

The cleat is optional. Two plywood layers glue to the back, and a metal Z-bar screws to the outer layer. The nightstand is light enough to hang. Lift it straight up to take it off the wall, and straight down to set it on the bar. The wall bar can stay up when you stand the nightstand back on its feet.

Each side is notched into two feet, so there is 86 mm clear under the sides and 100 mm under the shelf. The opening runs front to back, between the feet, which is room for a typical robot vacuum. Check the height of yours. A felt or rubber dot under each foot keeps a hard floor from getting scratched.

Design rule. Where a piece of furniture can reasonably hang, design it so a French cleat can hold it. The cleat parts ride along in the same sheet, and the floor-standing build still works if you leave them off.

Kitchen cart

A rolling cart from one sheet of 18 mm plywood. The worktop sits at counter height. An empty cart can park on a French cleat; a loaded one stays on its casters.

Dimensions

Overall 600 × 450 × 900 mm (23.6 × 17.7 × 35.4 in). Worktop at 900 mm.
Rail A 22 mm lip on the back and both sides, top at 922 mm. The front stays open.
Shelf Ledgers drop into a grid of slots, 50 mm apart, so the shelf can move. The drawer, if you build it, rides on the same kind of ledger.
Parts 29 plywood parts: 19 in the core cart, 10 optional (the drawer module and the cleat). Four casters are bought parts, shown here as simple wheels. Two test coupons are in the nest and are not drawn here.
Sheet One 1220 × 2440 mm sheet. The parts cover 79% of it.
Feet Two 18 mm layers at each corner. Caster plate on the bottom, base on the top. Base underside at 138 mm.

The sheet

Build

Glue is the joint. Spread water-resistant wood glue on every tab, slot, and finger face, then drive the screws so they clamp the joint while the glue cures. The screws stay in after that. Scrape or sand the char off the lasered faces first. Wipe the squeeze-out. Give it about an hour before you move it, and a day before you load it. Leave the ledgers and the shelf dry so you can lift them into another row later.

Pilot holes are 3.0 mm. Twenty #8 × 1½ in, thirteen #8 × 1¼ in, and twelve #8 × 1¾ in. Eight of the long screws hold the feet and four hold the cleat. A cart with no drawer and no cleat uses 31 screws. The casters take sixteen M8 × 30 bolts, with a nut and a washer on each.

  1. Cut the coupons, then the sheet. Dry-fit every part and clean the char out of the slots and off the tab faces.
  2. Bolt a caster to each foot plate, glue the nut-pocket layer over the nuts, and clamp. Do all four feet.
  3. Glue the feet under the base and drive the screws down through the base.
  4. Stand the sides on the base. Glue the bottom tabs into the base and the back tabs into both sides. Strap the box, screw the sides to the back, then drive the base screws up from below.
  5. Glue the top onto the side tabs and drive its screws.
  6. Glue the three rails around the top and screw them up from underneath. Leave the front open.
  7. Drop the shelf ledgers into the row you want and set the loose shelf on them. If you are building the drawer, assemble it the same way as the nightstand and set it on the upper ledgers.
  8. If you want the cleat, glue the backer on and fit the Z-bar. Fill the screw holes in the top, finish the wood, and lock the casters before you load the cart.
Cut the two test coupons first. The tab should slide through the slot with finger pressure and sit flush. A feed of 100–200 mm per minute at full power is only a starting guess, carried over from the nightstand. It is an unverified estimate for 18 mm plywood. Set the real speed from the coupons, and stay with the machine while it cuts.

Notes

The cleat is optional, and it is for parking an empty cart. A loaded cart stays on its casters. Two plywood layers glue to the back so a Z-bar can stand proud of the top and the base. Empty the cart, lift it onto the wall bar, and lock the casters. Lift it off when you want to roll it.

The underside of the base is 138 mm above the floor: the casters, plus the two plywood foot layers. The gap is open from the front and the back, so a robot vacuum can pass under. Between the front and back feet the side opening is shorter, so a robot comes in from the front or the back.

Design rule. Where a piece of furniture can reasonably hang, design it so a French cleat can hold it. On this cart that means an empty park on the wall. The cleat parts are in the same sheet, and the rolling build still works if you leave them off.
